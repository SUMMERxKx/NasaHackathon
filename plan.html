The plan

What we are building

Working name: EMBER, Evidence Map for Burning in Exploration Regimes. Tagline: What do we really know about fire where you are going? The team can change the name.

Who it is for

Maya is a materials engineer on a pressurized rover team. Her cabin will run at 34 percent oxygen and 8.2 psi. She needs to know which NASA flight experiments apply to her material choices and how far their results can be trusted at her conditions.

Today she would open 24 archive pages, download tables with broken labels, and build her own spreadsheet. With EMBER she picks her atmosphere and in ten seconds sees that the solid material tests all sit near cabin air, which experiments come closest, and what gas and droplet tests say at her oxygen level. Then she asks "what is missing for the Moon?" and reads a short answer with its sources.

The 30 second demo

  1. 0 to 3 s. A round blue flame. "In freefall, a flame becomes a sphere."
  2. 3 to 8 s. ISS cabin selected, coverage high. "EMBER reads 24 NASA investigations and about 2,000 tests."
  3. 8 to 15 s. Click Moon Base. The dial drops. "A Moon Base runs at 30 percent oxygen or more. Watch the evidence thin out."
  4. 15 to 22 s. The map. Gas and droplet dots near the Moon Base marker, no solid material dots. "Solid material tests stop near 22 percent. That gap is the finding."
  5. 22 to 28 s. Ask screen. An example answer appears, then a typed question streams a live answer with source chips. "A live model answers only from the NASA record, with its sources."
  6. 28 to 30 s. Name, tagline, link.

The demo must be 30 seconds or less. One second over and the project cannot be judged globally.

The finding

Where the evidence sits, and where it stops

NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository has a public data service. Each combustion investigation carries a small table with one row per test. This chart plots the tests that state both oxygen and pressure. It is the centrepiece screen of the app.

Solid materials stop near cabin air

BASS-II, the main solid material flammability study, runs from 14 to 22 percent oxygen. No solid fuel test in PSI reaches 30 percent, which is where a Moon Base is planned.

High oxygen evidence is gas and droplets

Everything at 30 percent oxygen or more comes from gas flames (452 rows, up to 85 percent) and 24 FLEX droplet tests.

A classic law checks out

The d-squared law for a burning droplet predicts burn time. Across 196 FLEX tests, predicted divided by measured burn time has a median of 0.95.

No partial gravity data

PSI holds no Moon or Mars gravity combustion tests yet. Microgravity and normal gravity results bound the answer, and the app says so.

Evidence Coverage score

Each test gets a weight that falls off with distance from the chosen atmosphere. Four points of oxygen, or about 25 percent in pressure, is one step away. Assumed values count half. The effective number of nearby tests N becomes a score of 100 × (1 minus e to the minus N over 10), so 10 nearby tests gives 63 and 30 gives 95. It counts evidence. It is not a safety rating, and the app says so wherever the score appears.

Relevance ranking

45 percent closeness of an investigation's own tests to the habitat, 25 percent fuel type (solids rank highest for crew cabins), 20 percent match to the chosen fire safety role, 10 percent amount of test data. Each card shows the four parts as bars. It is labelled as a transparent heuristic.

Live model design

A live model that can only repeat the NASA evidence

The Ask screen shows the evidence first, with no model. A live model then writes a short answer from that evidence. It tries GPT-5 mini on Azure first and a free Cloudflare model second. If both are slow or down, the evidence answer is already on screen.

How answers stay grounded

  • These rules apply to both models. The server accepts evidence ids only. It loads the text itself from a bundle built from the NASA snapshot, so the model is never shown anything else.
  • The instruction tells the model to use only that evidence, end every sentence with the id of its source, and copy numbers exactly without computing new ones.
  • If the evidence does not answer the question, the model must reply: "The PSI evidence in EMBER does not answer that."
  • The model may never call a habitat or material safe or unsafe.
  • The browser then checks the answer. A sentence that cites an unknown source, or contains a number that is not in the source it cites, is removed. If fewer than two sentences survive, the live answer is dropped.
  • The science lead tests it with ten real questions and three trick questions before the demo is recorded.

If the model fails or is slow

  • Evidence first. The evidence answer is on screen before the model is called, so there is never an empty screen.
  • Pinned answers. The six example questions appear at once and need no network.
  • Automatic switch. Any Azure error, or no first word from Azure within 6 seconds, and the same request goes to the Cloudflare model.
  • Time limits. No first word within 12 seconds, or no finished answer within 30 seconds, and the request is dropped.
  • When the credit ends. Azure starts refusing requests about 14 December. The Cloudflare model then answers everything.
  • Errors. If both models fail, the evidence answer stays with one quiet line.
  • The demo. The recording taps an example question first, then types one question to show the live stream.

Roles

One builder, one scientist, one designer, one storyteller

The build is one person's job because the coding assistant does the typing. The other three do the work that wins and that an assistant cannot be trusted with alone. Swap roles to fit the team's skills.

Person 1

Build lead

Runs the one-session build from the build brief, answers the assistant's questions, sets up the Azure free account and logs in to GitHub and Cloudflare when asked, applies fixes from the others, deploys the site and the live model endpoint, keeps the repository in order.

Person 2

Science and data lead

Confirms assumptions against the NASA records, recomputes insight numbers by hand, reviews the pinned answers and tests the live model, writes the explainers, owns the reference list, signs off every claim.

Person 3

Design and demo video lead

Sets the visual direction in the first hour, reviews the live site twice, storyboards, records and edits the 30 second video, captures screenshots.

Person 4

Story, slides and submission lead

Owns the project page, the story, the seven slides, the SI disclosure text, the clock, and pressing Submit early and again at the end.
